# How have female and male contributing family worker rates evolved relative to one another in the Democratic Republic of Congo between 1991 and 2023?

## Abstract

Using World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI), this study examines the evolution of female and male contributing family worker rates in the Democratic Republic of Congo between 1991 and 2023. The analysis compares two key indicators: the percentage of female contributing family workers (% of female employment) and the percentage of male contributing family workers (% of male employment). Over this thirty-two-year period, the data show a marked shift in labor composition: initially, most of the female workforce was engaged as contributing family workers, while male participation was comparatively low. Over time, female rates decreased significantly, while male rates increased moderately. This convergence reflects structural changes in labor dynamics, yet female contribution remains moderately higher than male. These patterns illustrate the gradual balancing of gender participation in family labor and provide insights into employment trends, social transformation, and economic diversification in the DRC.

## 1. Question

How have female and male contributing family worker rates evolved relative to one another in the Democratic Republic of Congo between 1991 and 2023?

- **Female proxy**: Contributing family workers, female (% of female employment) (modeled ILO estimate)
- **Male proxy**: Contributing family workers, male (% of male employment) (modeled ILO estimate)

## 2. Data

- **Source**: World Bank World Development Indicators (WDI)
- **Indicators**:
  - Contributing family workers, female (% of female employment) (modeled ILO estimate)
  - Contributing family workers, male (% of male employment) (modeled ILO estimate)
- **Coverage**: Democratic Republic of Congo, 1991–2023
- **Notes**: National-level data only

## 3. Method

1. Filtered dataset for the Democratic Republic of Congo and selected the two contributing family worker indicators.
2. **Extracted relevant columns**: Year, Indicator Name, and Value.
3. Pivoted the dataset to create a side-by-side comparison of female and male contributing family worker rates between 1991 and 2023.
4. Generated a dual-line time series plot to visualize gender differences, convergence trends, and overall shifts in family labor participation.

(Analysis is descriptive; no causal inference applied.)

## 4. Results

- **Female contributing family workers**: Began the period at a high level, reflecting that a majority of women were engaged in family labor. Over time, rates decreased significantly, showing a marked decline in female-dominated family work.
- **Male contributing family workers**: Started at comparatively low levels but increased moderately over the period, indicating gradual male integration into family labor activities.
- **Comparison**: The two indicators show converging trends over time, with female rates still moderately higher than male rates by 2023. The movement reflects a narrowing gender gap in family labor participation, with a clear shift toward more balanced gender distribution.

## 5. Interpretation

- The declining share of female contributing family workers alongside rising male participation suggests structural shifts in the labor force, possibly linked to urbanization, sectoral diversification, and changing household labor dynamics.
- The convergence of the two lines indicates a gradual reduction of gender disparities in family labor, though women still dominate this category.
- These trends may reflect broader socio-economic transitions, including increased female access to wage employment and male involvement in family enterprises.
- Overall, the data highlight ongoing changes in employment patterns and the evolving role of gender within household and informal labor markets.

## 6. Limitations

- National-level aggregates may conceal regional or sector-specific differences, including urban-rural disparities.
- Modeled ILO estimates may contain uncertainty, particularly in earlier years with limited survey coverage.
- The descriptive approach does not determine causal factors behind the decline in female participation or the rise in male contribution, such as policy changes, labor market incentives, or social norms.

## 7. Next Steps / Extensions

- Disaggregate by sector (agriculture, informal enterprises) to better understand the contexts driving gendered labor shifts.
- Explore correlations with macroeconomic trends, education levels, and female labor force participation in formal sectors.
- Compare DRC’s trends with other Central African countries to examine regional patterns of gendered family labor.
- Conduct time-series analysis to separate long-term trends from short-term fluctuations and identify structural changes in household labor dynamics.
